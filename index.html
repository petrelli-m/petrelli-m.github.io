    About

    I am a petrologist and volcanologist working on igneous systems, quantitative petrology, volcanic hazard assessment, and the application of machine-learning methods to Earth science. I obtained my PhD in 2006.

    After several years in academia, which I greatly enjoyed, family circumstances led me to step away from academic work in 2009 to care for my three young daughters, then aged 1, 1, and 3. During this period, I gained professional experience in e-marketing, web development, and e-commerce, where I was exposed to the early broad adoption of machine learning in these fields. When I returned to academia in 2014, this experience helped me shape a research line focused on applying machine learning to high-dimensional petrological and geochemical datasets.

    Since 2002 (with a pause from 2009 to 2014), I have also developed and managed a LA-ICP-MS laboratory that supports research in tephrochronology, magma dynamics, and crustal evolution. I devote significant effort to teaching and training through university courses, international short courses, and two books on Python and machine learning for Earth scientists.

    Over the past decade, I have published more than 100 articles in leading international scientific journals, as well as two books, and over the last five years, I have secured approximately €6 million in competitive research funding from European and local sources. I conceived and proudly coordinate the MSCA REALISE Doctoral Network and REALI3SE Staff Exchange projects, two major collaborative initiatives in igneous petrology. They are designed to connect igneous petrologists and volcanologists with data scientists and leading machine learning experts worldwide, to address timely and challenging problems in hazard assessment, risk mitigation, and the emplacement of fertile critical raw material deposits.

    I have been invited to speak at international conferences and leading academic institutions on machine learning in petrology and volcanology. In my spare time, I enjoy spending time with my family, looking after my dog, and reading about the philosophy of science.

    Research

    Machine learning in petrology, geochemistry and volcanology

    I aim to systematically investigate the use of machine learning (ML) techniques for the solution of problems that are relevant to geological applications, with emphasis on igneous petrology, geochemistry, and volcanology. This line of research was sparked in 2014 thanks to the grants “Challenge: Coupling macHine leArning, Leading anaLytical mEthods aNd larGe gEochemical datasets in tephra studies” (PI, 2015–2017) and “ENGAGE: machinE learNinG Applications for Geological problEms” (PI, 2019–2020), funded by the University of Perugia, and the Microsoft Machine Learning Award (PI, 2015). It is now supported by many active grants that I lead or act as co-PI.

    The work has developed through a series of connected studies. It began with classification methods applied to geochemical data for tectonic setting recognition, was later extended to volcanology and tephrochronology, and then progressed to regression approaches for estimating magmatic pressure and temperature as a complement to established thermodynamic methods. These developments were synthesised in a broader review of the field, and I am currently contributing a book chapter on methodological advances and the growing role of artificial intelligence in volcanology.

    Dynamics and timescales of volcanic plumbing systems

    This line of research focuses on the geochemical, petrological, and volcanological characterisation of magmatic systems, with particular emphasis on estimating the timescales of magmatic processes that occur in volcanic plumbing systems before eruptions. To do that, I combine numerical methods, experimental petrology, and the study of natural samples. The main aim is to provide methods and tools that support public authorities and decision-makers in volcanic hazard assessment and risk mitigation.

    REALISE MSCA Doctoral Network

    Bridging igneous petrology and machine learning for science and society. REALISE explores the opportunities, challenges and risks of artificial intelligence in igneous petrology, and trains Earth scientists and entrepreneurs to use machine learning to acquire, model and interpret petrological data.     Books

    2023

    Machine Learning for Earth Sciences Using Python to Solve Geological Problems

    This textbook introduces the reader to machine learning applications in Earth Sciences. It starts with the basics of machine learning and its potential to solve geological problems, describes the main Python tools for ML and the typical workflow of ML applications in Earth Sciences, and explains how the algorithms work. It gives many worked examples: clustering and dimensionality reduction in petro-volcanological studies, clustering of multi-spectral data, well-log facies classification, and machine-learning regression in petrology. It also introduces parallel computing and how to scale ML models in the cloud. The book is for Earth scientists at any level, from students to academics and professionals.

    Petrelli M. (2023). Machine Learning for Earth Sciences: Using Python to Solve Geological Problems. Springer Textbooks in Earth Sciences, Geography and Environment. Springer Cham. DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-35114-3

    2021

    Introduction to Python in Earth Science Data Analysis From Descriptive Statistics to Machine Learning

    This textbook introduces Python programming for exploring and modelling data in the Earth Sciences. It takes the reader from the very first steps, setting up the environment and writing the first lines of code, to confident use of Python for visualising, analysing and modelling Earth Science data. Every chapter contains worked code examples, each commented in detail. It is written for complete beginners and can be used in master’s and PhD courses; early-career and experienced researchers who want to start using Python for geological problems will also benefit from it.

    Petrelli M. (2021). Introduction to Python in Earth Science Data Analysis: From Descriptive Statistics to Machine Learning. Springer Textbooks in Earth Sciences, Geography and Environment. Springer Cham. DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-78055-5
